4 · Leverage

Source: pages 7–9

Concept

Leverage = using fixed cost to increase profit.

Operating Leverage (OL)

Using Operating Fixed Cost to increase profit. Operating FC = Admin / Selling / Distribution.

Financial Leverage (FL)

Using Financial Fixed Cost to increase profit. Financial FC = Interest & Pref dividend(1 − t)

Preference dividend is an after-tax figure; interest is before-tax. To compare, gross up the pref dividend: after tax = 100 (1 − 0.3), so before tax = 100(1 − 0.3).

% Method = % where you reach% where you start

OL = % change in EBIT% change in Sale
FL = % change in EBT (or EAT or EPS)% change in EBIT

Ratio Method = 1st profit (upper)2nd profit (lower)

OL = ContributionEBIT
FL — only interest = EBITEBT   or   EBITEBIT − Interest
FL — interest + pref dividend = EBITEBIT − Interest − Pd(1 − t)
Combined Leverage (CL) = OL × FL

Never ever in life…

  • OL & FL are not restricted between 0 & 1.
  • They can be less than 0 (negative).
  • They can be more than 1.
OL = 1Margin of Safety ratio (MOS)
Margin of Safety = Actual Sale − BEP Sale = ProfitPV ratio

Most important thing — calculating EBIT

EBIT is not changing & given

Simply given in the question.

EBIT is changing

  • ROI on new capital is constant — new EBIT = new total capital × existing ROI (ROCE)
  • ROI on new capital is a specific % given in question — new EBIT = new total capital × that %

Example — ROI on new capital is constant

Current EBIT ₹5L, current equity ₹15L, current debt ₹25L.

Current ROI / ROCE = 515 + 25 = 12.5%

Raise ₹20L → New total capital = 15L + 25L + 20L = ₹60L. ROI = ROCE = 12.5%.

New EBIT = 60L × 12.5% = ₹7.5 lakh

Example — ROI on new capital is 10%

Same figures; new total capital = ₹60L; ROI on new capital = 10% (given).

New EBIT = 60L × 10% = ₹6 lakh

Please keep in mind: if P/E ratio (MPS / EPS) is given, also calculate MPS.

6 · Cost of Capital

Source: pages 13–18

Meaning

When you go to the market to raise capital, the amount paid to the owners of capital is called the cost of capital. It equals the minimum rate of return expected by the owners of the capital.

Three costs: Ke (cost of equity), Kp (cost of preference), Kd (cost of debt).

Irredeemable — no fixed life

Here we don't repay the original amount — only pay interest or dividend. General rule: Cost = Given (paid)Received (मिला)

Ke = Equity dividendEquity share cap (NP)   →   P0 = DKe
Kp = Pref dividendPref share cap (NP)
Kd = Interest (1 − t)Debt (NP)

Redeemable — fixed life

Here we pay both — interest / dividend and the main amount at the end of the fixed life.

Kd = Interest (1 − t) + RV − NPlifeRV + NP2
Kp = Pref dividend + RV − NPlifeRV + NP2

Most important — RV, NP, IP, MPS, FV

  • RV = Redemption Value = amount paid at the last. If nothing is given, RV = 100.
  • NP = Net proceeds, IP = Issue price, MPS = Market price, FV = Face Value.
  • Issue price / market price = price the customer / stock market will pay.

  • Only face value & flotation cost given → NP = FV − flotation cost %

Redeemable — Convertible Debenture / Preference (life fixed)

When there is an option to convert debt or preference into equity at the end of life:

Convertible Kd = Interest (1 − t) + RV − NPlifeRV + NP2
Convertible Kp = Pref dividend + RV − NPlifeRV + NP2

For RV, choose the higher of:

  • Redemption amount, OR
  • (No. of equity shares received for 1 debenture / preference) × (price of equity on the last redemption date)

Cost of Equity (Ke) — approaches

1. Dividend Discount (basic) model

Ke = DNP or P0 or MPS   →   P0 = DKe

2. Dividend Growth model

P0 = D0(1 + g)Ke% − g% = D1Ke% − g%
Growth (g) = b × r   where   b = Retained EPSEPS (retention ratio),   r = ROI or ROE

3. CAPM

Ke = Rf + [Rm − Rf] × β
  • Rf = Risk-free return (T-Bill return / Govt Bond return)
  • Rm = market return
  • Rm − Rf = market risk premium

4. Earning-yield ("worst") method

Ke = EPSMPS

Ke and Kre — relationship

Actual equity & R/S are almost the same, so generally Ke = Kre.

Ke = D1IP − flotation cost + g%
Kre = D1IP + g%

If flotation cost is given, reduce it from IP to get NP for Ke. Retained earnings carry no flotation cost, so Kre uses IP directly.

10 · Capital Structure Theories

Source: pages 25–26 (notes end mid-topic)

Approaches

  • Assumptions
  • Net Income (NI) Approach
  • Net Operating Income (NOI) Approach — with tax / without tax
  • Arbitrage
  • MM Approach — with tax / without tax

Assumptions

  1. Capital = Equity + Debt only (no Preference, no R/S).
  2. Total assets are fixed: Equity + Debt = same total (e.g. 50 + 50 = 100). Want more equity → sell debt (70 + 30 = 100). Want more debt → sell equity (20 + 80 = 100).
  3. Life = ∞ (Irredeemable) — use irredeemable formulae.
  4. Entire EATESH is distributed as dividend → EATESH = equity dividend, so R/S = 0.

Levered vs Unlevered firm

Levered Firm

  • Has both equity & debt.
  • Value of Equity (Lev) = EATESHKe (Lev)
  • Value of Debt is always given.
  • Value of Firm = Value of Debt (Lev) + Value of Equity (Lev)
  • Ko = WACC of Ke & Kd

Unlevered Firm

  • Has only equity, no debt.
  • Value of Equity = EATESHKe
  • Value of Equity = Value of Firm
  • Ke = Ko

Net Income (NI) Approach

Under NI, Ke and Kd stay constant (given values) regardless of the debt–equity mix — ignore any change in them. As cheaper debt increases, Ko falls and the value of the firm rises; optimum structure = maximum debt.
